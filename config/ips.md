## 1. Goals

In plain terms: I want two separate buckets of stock picks. Bucket A is
"steady compounders" — solid businesses I expect to grow around 15–22%
a year. Bucket B is "swing for the fences" — higher-risk picks I'm
specifically hunting for because they could double or triple in value,
and I expect them to grow around 26–41% a year if they work out. Every
idea in either bucket has to pass checks based on the methods of
thirteen well-known, named investors before I take it seriously.

Track A ideas are judged over 5+ years (did they beat the index?).
Track B ideas are judged over 2–3 years (did they double or triple?).

## 4. Sizing and hard limits

I accept the following sizing rules:

- The active sleeve will begin at 5% of total investable assets.
- A fully sized Track A position may represent up to 2.5% of total
  investable assets.
- A fully sized Track B position may represent up to 1.25% of total
  investable assets.
- No single company may represent more than 25% of the active sleeve.
- No sector may represent more than 30% of the active sleeve.
- Small-cap companies in aggregate may represent no more than 35% of
  the active sleeve.
- Track B in aggregate may represent no more than 40% of the active
  sleeve.
- Where two limits conflict, the more restrictive limit applies.
- I will not use leverage, margin, futures and options, or derivatives.

At the initial 5% active-sleeve stage, the 25% single-company limit
restricts each position to 1.25% of total investable assets. Therefore,
the 2.5% Track A target is not available at this stage. It becomes
available only when the active sleeve reaches at least 10%, and only if
all other limits remain satisfied.

## 5. Sell discipline

I will not sell or trim a position merely because it has doubled or reached another arbitrary return target. A 2x return is evidence that a Track B hypothesis may be working, not an automatic sell signal. I will allow winners to continue compounding while the original thesis remains intact and the valuation remains defensible.

I will sell only when the investment thesis has materially broken, the market price has moved far beyond a defensible valuation, or a materially better opportunity exists while my available capital is already fully deployed. A falling share price by itself is not a reason to sell. I will not average down when the thesis is broken.

I will not sell any position within 12 months of purchase unless the thesis has broken. Valuation concerns arising during the first 12 months may stop me from adding, but will not override this minimum holding period by themselves.

## 6. Review cadence

I will review the per-track scorecards quarterly, using post-tax results against both components of the frozen benchmark. These quarterly reviews are for monitoring evidence and rule compliance, not for reacting to short-term price movements.

Once a year, I will re-underwrite every position against its original thesis, valuation, pre-mortem, and kill criteria. I will review advancement through the allocation stages only when the relevant evidence threshold has been reached: after 12 months, after 24 months with at least 15 aged decisions, and after four years with at least 30 aged decisions and attributable evidence of an edge.

Track A and Track B will be evaluated separately. I will shut down Track B if it trails either frozen benchmark post-tax over its 2–3-year evaluation window. I will shut down Track A if it trails either benchmark post-tax over its 5-year evaluation window. Failure in one track will not be concealed by success in the other. A shut-down track will receive no new purchases, and its existing positions will be wound down under the normal sell discipline rather than sold indiscriminately.

Any rule breach will trigger the automatic demotion required by the scorecard. Two stage demotions will end the active process rather than prompt me to commit more capital.

## 7. What would make you stop using this application entirely

I accept that disciplined research software does not guarantee investment skill. The application can improve coverage, consistency, detection speed, and record-keeping, but it cannot ensure that my judgments about businesses are correct.

I will regard isolated losses, a market drawdown, or a short period of underperformance as normal variation rather than proof that the process has failed. However, if the active sleeve is still behind either component of its frozen benchmark post-tax at the end of Year 5, I will treat that as evidence that the process has failed to add value. I will stop making new active investments through Artha and wind the active sleeve into the passive core.

I will also stop earlier if I incur two stage demotions, consistently fail to produce or read decision-grade dossiers, repeatedly breach the approved-dossier requirement, or cannot attribute apparent outperformance to a named source and track. Unexplained outperformance will be treated as luck, not as evidence that the application deserves more capital.
